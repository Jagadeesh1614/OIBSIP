Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

The scientist who taught a nation to dream.

A life of service

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, into a modest family. As a boy he sold newspapers to help at home, and he studied by lamplight. His curiosity about birds in flight was the first spark of a career in aerospace.

After studying physics and aerospace engineering, he joined India's space and defence research organisations. He led the team behind India's first indigenous satellite launch vehicle and later guided the country's missile programme, earning the affectionate title "Missile Man of India".

In 2002 he became India's 11th President, and people called him the "People's President". He spent much of his term speaking with students and teachers, convinced that the young held the country's future in their hands.

Kalam remained a teacher until his last day. He passed away in 2015 while delivering a lecture to students, doing what he loved most. His humility, discipline and belief in young people still inspire millions.
